The Game of Backgammon

Backgammon is a popular board game, providing an intriguing combination of skill and luck. Its essentially modern form first arose in Persia during the 6th century CE. Its most recent innovation, the doubling cube, was introduced in the 1920s. However, its origins date back some 5,000 years.

Using the software described here you can:

  1. Play the game against another human being via the web. The two of you may reside on different continents!
  2. Play the game against any of nine built-in AI brains.
  3. Set up the board and analyze any position.
  4. Design your own AI and evolve it via a genetic algorithm.
  5. Run a tournament among AIs you design or choose from the existing list.

The Game Board

In the default view of the game board, as shown in Figure 1, the home board of White is in the lower right quadrant, and White's outer board is in the lower left. Similarly, Red's home and outer boards are on the top, on the right and left, respectively. Red moves from the white home board to the red home board and White moves in the opposite direction. Both players move from their opponent's home board to their own home board, and from there to the collecting tray. The points of the board are numbered from 1 to 24. Red moves clockwise, and white moves counterclockwise. The bar contains any checkers that have been forced off the board by the opponent, and the collection tray next to the home boards receives all checkers that have been born off. It is sometimes convenient to think of the bar as point 25 for White and 0 for red, and the collection tray as point 0 for white and 25 for Red. With these conventions, Red always moves from some point to a point with a larger index, White moves to a point with a smaller index. The default view is Red's view as it would be on a physical board. That view is consistent with the diagrams shown in external external rules page.
